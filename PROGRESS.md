## Decisions & gotchas
- 2026-09-11 — **SPEC's bold-font heading test cannot be implemented, and was replaced.** `getTextContent` exposes `styles[].fontFamily`, but pdf.js sets that from `font.fallbackName`, which is only ever `"serif"` / `"sans-serif"` / `"monospace"` (worker source, `fallbackName` assignment). The real font name never reaches the text layer, so `/bold|black|semibold/i` can never match. Substituted the same signal by another route: a heading is set in a face that is **not the document's body face**, where the body face is the modal `fontName` weighted by character count (`modalFontName`). This is strictly stronger in practice — it caught every subsection heading in BERT and Attention, all of which are set at *exactly* body height and which the height test alone misses completely.
- 2026-09-11 — **The height test alone would have found only top-level headings, and barely.** BERT's body is 10.9pt and "1 Introduction" is 12.0pt, against a 1.1× threshold of 11.99 — a 0.01pt margin. Every `2.1`-style subsection is 10.9pt, i.e. identical to body. Do not weaken the font test on the assumption height is a backstop; it is not.
- 2026-09-11 — **SPEC's heading prefix regex was tightened.** `/^(\d+(\.\d+)*|[IVXL]+)[.)\s]/` matches the citation years that begin a wrapped body line ("2018). These include sentence-level tasks…") and percentages ("80.5% (7.7% point absolute…"). Section numbers are one or two digits per level followed by whitespace, optionally after a dot or paren: `/^(\d{1,2}(\.\d{1,2}){0,3}|[IVXL]{1,5})[.)]?\s+\S/`. Cut BERT's candidates from 62 to 38 before any other test.
- 2026-09-11 — **A heading is never set smaller than body text** (floor at 0.95 × body). Bold tick labels and legends inside plots otherwise pass every other test — "0.6 SGDNesterov+dropout" in Adam and "10 10 20-layer" in ResNet are chart furniture at 5–7pt against a 10pt body. Also requires a run of two or more letters, which kills pure-numeric table rows like "94.9 60.5 86.5 89.3".
- 2026-09-11 — **The References cutoff is applied to the line stream, not to the assembled sections**, so it holds on the bookmark path too. Bookmarks routinely omit a References entry: before this, Attention's final bookmarked section swallowed the whole bibliography and came to 10,564 characters against 1,216 after. Not gated on the heading test either — a line reading exactly "References" is the heading whatever face it is in. Note GPT-3 puts References on page 68, *after* its appendices, and the cutoff correctly keeps appendices A–H.
- 2026-09-11 — **Sections with empty text are kept deliberately.** A parent heading whose first subsection follows it immediately ("3. Deep Residual Learning", then "3.1. Residual Learning"; Attention's "Results") has no prose of its own. Dropping them was tried and makes the outline read as though the grouping did not exist. **Step 7 must skip the model call for a section with no text** rather than send an empty one — CLAUDE.md's "2–4 bullets per section" cannot apply to a section with nothing in it, and under `per-section` an empty request is also a wasted one.
- 2026-09-11 — Line text is de-hyphenated across line breaks ("representa-" + "tion" → "representation") when the break follows a lowercase letter. Not in SPEC, but the text goes to a model and hyphen fragments cost quality for three lines of code.
- 2026-09-11 — Fixtures in `fixtures/` are 628 KB for eleven papers, GPT-3 alone 210 KB of it. Kept whole rather than truncated: step 7 needs realistic section lengths, and GPT-3 is the useful stress case at 32 sections against the 40 cap. `fixtures/README.md` records provenance and which path produced each. Regenerate them whenever extraction changes.
- 2026-09-11 — Both synthetic test PDFs (a low-text one for the scanned branch, a headingless one for the chunk fallback) are generated by hand-written Python in the scratchpad — no ghostscript or ImageMagick on this machine. Worth rebuilding rather than hunting for a real scan if those branches need retesting.
- 2026-09-11 — **SPEC's two-column acceptance test is not sufficient and was tightened.** Centre gap > 0.15 × page width plus both clusters ≥ 20% of items accepts *single-column* papers: justified body text spreads item midpoints widely enough that 2-means splits it into left and right halves with centres ~0.35 × page width apart. Measured false positives on Attention (14/15 pages) and Adam (15/15). Added a third condition: the share of items whose horizontal span **straddles** the proposed gutter must be ≤ 5%. A run crossing the gutter is proof there is no gutter. The two specified conditions still apply unchanged; this one only rejects.
- 2026-09-11 — **The column verdict is per document, not per page.** Straddle share per page overlaps badly between layouts (BERT has a 13% page, Adam has a 2% page), because two-column papers have full-width table pages and single-column papers have pages of centred equations. A majority vote across pages separates cleanly with room to spare: the weakest two-column paper polls 8/13 and the strongest single-column one 4/15. `layoutDocument` decides once and every page uses that verdict; `columnVerdict` also returns the mean gutter position as a fraction of page width, which is steadier than any single page's.
- 2026-09-11 — **Full-width lines break the page into bands**, a deliberate refinement of SPEC's "all of the left column top-to-bottom, then the right". Items straddling the gutter are their own group (`column: -1`); column lines above each spanning line are emitted before it. Without this the BERT title sorts *after* the entire left column, because its midpoint sits near the page centre and lands in whichever cluster is closer. Also fixes full-width tables and captions mid-page. Verified: BERT page 1 now reads title → authors → affiliation → abstract.
- 2026-09-11 — **Sideways text runs are dropped** unless they are the majority on the page. The arXiv stamp down BERT's left margin is rotated 90° on an unrotated page, so its baseline crosses every body line and pdf.js hands it back at a y that splices it into the middle of a paragraph. Detected per item (`|t[1]| > |t[0]|` after the viewport transform), not per page — `viewport.rotation` only sees a declared `/Rotate`. If sideways runs are the *majority* the page is genuinely rotated without declaring it, so everything is kept rather than throwing the page away. The count surfaces in the debug panel.
- 2026-09-11 — Line assembly moved to its own `extract/lines.js` rather than growing `columns.js` past the 200-line rule. Spacing between runs is reconstructed from geometry (gap > 0.2 × item height) because pdf.js is inconsistent about whether a run carries its own trailing space.
- 2026-09-11 — **Extraction is verifiable without a browser.** pdf.js runs in Node via `pdfjs-dist/legacy/build/pdf.mjs`; a scratchpad harness bundles the real `src/extract/*` modules with esbuild, aliasing `viewer/pdfjs.js` to a Node shim (`pdf.worker.mjs` must sit next to the bundle). This is how step 5 was verified and how step 6's heading detection should be iterated — far faster than reloading the extension for each change. Harness and the five test PDFs live in the session scratchpad, not the repo.
- 2026-09-11 — `modalHeight` is a plain mode over item heights bucketed to 0.1pt, as specified, *not* weighted by character count. Weighting was considered — it would make body text dominate more strongly — but the literal reading was kept so step 6's body-size rule and this one cannot drift apart. Measured body heights look right: 9.7–10.9pt across the five test papers.
- 2026-09-11 — **Reading position restores exactly only when the layout is identical.** `docs` records `scrollHeight` alongside `lastPage`/`scrollTop`; on reopen the raw `scrollTop` is used when `scrollHeight` matches, otherwise it falls back to the page number. A different window width rescales every offset, so a raw restore would land at random. The extra field is a deliberate addition to SPEC.md's `docs` shape.
- 2026-09-11 — `pdfview.load()` now takes **bytes, not a URL**; `fetchPdf` is exported and the viewer calls it itself. The hash must be computed **before** `getDocument`, which may transfer the buffer to the worker and leave it detached.
