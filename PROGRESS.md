## Running the live probe

The Gemini key is at `~/.config/scholar-reader/gemini.key` (outside the repo, mode 600). `npm run probe` sends a fixture to a real provider and reports section alignment, bullet counts, over-length bullets by position, and whether results sections keep their figures:

```
npm run probe                                        # adam / gemini-dev, per-section, ~0.1c
npm run probe -- --fixture bert --provider gemini-prod   # whole-document, ~1c
```

`tools/live-probe.mjs` is the only thing that reads that key path; it never prints it. The extension itself takes its key from settings into `storage.local` at step 14.

## Decisions & gotchas
- 2026-09-12 — **Lines are grouped on the body baseline, not on the first item's y — subscripts were being stranded as lines of their own.** Found on a real paper and reproduced synthetically: items sort by `y`, so a raised superscript *opens* the group, and a subscript on the same visual line is then further from that anchor (5.5pt at 10pt body) than the 0.4 × body tolerance allows. It became a line in its own right and landed in the text away from the words it belonged to — garbled formulas, and plausibly also why `⊙` and `∝` looked wrong, since operators live in exactly the lines that carry scripts. `lines.js` now tracks the tallest item's y as the group's baseline and gives a run markedly smaller than the line (< 0.85 ×) 1.6 × the reach. The size test is what makes it safe: two runs of body text are never small relative to each other, so no two body lines can merge however close they are. ⚠️ **Not re-verified against the eleven fixtures** — those are assembled sections, not raw text-layer items, so this path cannot be replayed from them; the guard cases (body lines at 5pt, a smaller footnote line, a 9pt/10pt size change) are tested synthetically instead.
- 2026-09-12 — **Font coverage was measured, not guessed** (`fontTools` over the 583 installed font files). The SF faces behind `system-ui` cover no math operators: `⊙` U+2299 and `∝` U+221D are both absent. STIX Two Math has `⊙` but **not** `∝` — that one is in STIXVariants — so stack order matters; Apple Symbols has both, ships with macOS, and blends with SF, so it goes first, with Arial Unicode MS as the backstop. This is a safety net only: characters that are *stylings* of letters are still flattened by `extract/normalize.js`, because no font stack fixes a codepoint nothing renders well.
- 2026-09-12 — **Math glyphs are flattened to plain letters at extraction (`extract/normalize.js`), not papered over with a font.** Found live: `𝜈` vanished and `𝑎,𝑏` drew as hex boxes. pdf.js maps a paper's math-italic glyphs into Mathematical Alphanumeric Symbols (U+1D400–U+1D7FF) — confirmed against the reported characters, U+1D708/U+1D44E/U+1D44F — and no UI font on macOS covers that plane-1 block. Each character there is a *styling* of a plain letter and its NFKC form is that letter, which every font has and which loses nothing in a two-line summary. **Not a blanket NFKC**, deliberately: that also flattens `10⁻⁸` → `10-8` and `½`, and results bullets are required to keep their reported numbers — the test asserts those survive. Applied at `textlayer.js`'s single `str:` assignment so titles, section text, the confirm card and the model payload are all fixed at once, again on model output (a model can re-emit the notation), and folded into `align.js`'s title checksum so a re-stylised echo cannot fail a whole response. A math-capable font stack is appended in `theme.css` as a safety net for anything outside the block; the normaliser is the fix. **None of the eleven fixtures contain these characters** — producer-dependent, so the fixture set is not evidence either way and this only shows up on real papers.
- 2026-09-12 — **A jump lands the heading ~30 PDF points below the top, not flush against it.** Found in the live run: arriving with the heading jammed into the top edge reads as a cut-off page rather than as the start of a section. The margin is in PDF points so it keeps its proportion to the text as the zoom changes, and because +y is up it is *added* to the target y. Near the top of a page that overshoots into the page before, which is what `allowNegativeOffset` is already there for and is the right thing to show. The flash still marks the heading itself, and the scroll-spy still measures the raw y — a section 30pt below the top is far above the spy's 30% line, so a jump still makes its own section the current one.
- 2026-09-12 — **The extraction panel stays expanded below the outline.** Offered to collapse it once the outline pane became real; the user's call is that it is intended and stays. It is development scaffolding on purpose and step 14 is where the reading chrome gets settled.
- 2026-09-12 — **A bullet jumps to its section, not to itself.** CLAUDE.md gives every bullet the `page`/`y` of its section and nothing finer, so a bullet is a way into a section rather than a location of its own. Clicking a bullet and clicking its heading are therefore the same jump, and the test asserts exactly that — if bullets ever carry their own targets, this is the assumption that has to be revisited first.
- 2026-09-12 — **Section offsets are measured fresh on every frame, never cached.** Zoom, a pane resize and a pdf.js re-render each move every offset at once, so a cache would have to be invalidated by all three and would be wrong in the window before it was. Forty `convertToViewportPoint` calls per animation frame is nothing next to the scroll that triggered them.
- 2026-09-12 — **`-1` is a real value for the current section.** Above the first heading no section is the one being read, and marking the first one anyway would be a small lie told constantly. `activeIndex` is a pure function precisely so this case is pinned by a test rather than by eyeballing a scroll.
- 2026-09-12 — **A section that failed, one that is empty, and one still being written must render three different ways** — the step-7 note about the live probe generalised to the UI. `outline-list.js` branches on `error` first, then on the `partial` flag, then falls through to the structural case ("no text of its own"). Under progressive fill a bullet-less section is *pending*, not empty, which is why the flag exists at all rather than being inferred from the bullet count.
- 2026-09-12 — **The pane and the scroll-spy each need the other, and `viewer.js` breaks the cycle with a `let`.** The pane sends the reader to a place in the paper; where the reader is sends the pane back a section. The spy is constructed first and reaches the pane through a binding assigned on the next line, before any scroll event can fire — and the pane's constructor already calls `onSections([])`, which the spy answers with an empty measurement rather than a call into an undefined pane.
- 2026-09-12 — **Every state change tells the spy the targets are gone.** `show()` clears them, so a highlight can never be applied to an outline that is no longer rendered — the bug this shape prevents is the error state or the confirm card quietly receiving `setActive` for sections that left the screen.

- 2026-09-11 — **Resolved (was the open question for step 7/13): `gemini-prod` keeps `strategy: "whole-document"`.** The original reason for it — a 20-requests-per-day cap — never existed, so the choice was re-argued from scratch against the eleven fixtures (mean paper 18 sections / ~13.2k est. tokens; `gpt3.json` the stress case at 32 sections / ~51k). `per-section` was recommended first, on three grounds, and two and a half of them did not survive scrutiny: (a) long-output degradation is modest, and everything structural about it is expressible in the JSON schema, which is constrained decoding rather than a request; (b) the index-misalignment hazard is fixable for a few lines — echo the title, keep using the index, treat a mismatch as malformed — so it is not a reason to pick a strategy; (c) cross-section context, which was the headline argument *for* whole-document, is close to worthless for bullets that CLAUDE.md requires to be extractive, since synthesis is exactly what the prompt forbids.
